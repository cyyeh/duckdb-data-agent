Distributions & outliers

Age distribution (incl. missing)

The age histogram shows the dataset is concentrated in young adults, with a sizable missing-age bucket:

  • 20–29 is the largest observed bin (220 passengers)
  • 30–39 next (167)
  • Missing age is also large (177)

Summary stats (non-missing ages):

  • Min: 0.42, Max: 80.0
  • Median: ~28.19, Avg: ~29.70
  • 25th–75th percentile: ~20.35 to 38.33

Where Age is missing (a data quality “shape” issue)

Missing age is not evenly distributed:

  • By sex: male 21.5% missing vs female 16.9%
  • By class: 3rd class 27.7% missing (highest), 2nd class 6.0% (lowest), 1st class 13.9%

That means any age-based analysis can be biased toward the groups with better-recorded ages (notably 2nd class).

Fare distribution (and outliers)

The fare chart (quartile-ish bins + a zero bin) shows fares are fairly spread across low-to-mid ranges, with a heavy right tail.

Fare summary stats:

  • Min: 0.0
  • 25th percentile: ~7.91
  • Median: ~14.45
  • 75th percentile: ~30.94
  • Mean: ~32.20 (higher than median → right-skew)
  • Max: 512.33 (very large outlier)

Outlier notes:

  • The very top fare (512.33) appears multiple times and shares the same ticket (PC 17755), suggesting a group/party ticketing pattern rather than independent purchases.
  • Zero fare occurs 15 times (1.7% of passengers). That’s worth sanity-checking (e.g., special cases, crew-like records, ticketing artifacts).
